## 1. Executive Summary

This analysis examines the full landscape of licensing requirements affecting providers across New York State's regulatory and sub-regulatory documentation. The data reveals a licensing ecosystem characterized by **overlapping credential systems, undefined standards applied systematically across professions, and administrative overhead that compounds at every level of the provider hierarchy**.

### Key Findings

- **No portable background check system** exists across NY health and human services. A nurse moving from a hospital to a home care agency triggers an entirely new criminal history record check, even if one was completed months prior.
- **"Good moral character"** — an undefined, standardless licensing criterion — appears in **25+ separate professional licensing regulations** across NYCRR Titles 1, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 12, and 18. None reference Article 23-A's legally required balancing test for criminal history considerations.
- **NYC creates a distinct licensing layer** on top of state requirements for child care, camps, after-school programs, and children's institutions, with separate permits, staffing standards, and training cycles that duplicate state frameworks.
- **County data is not independently maintained** in our database; county requirements are embedded in state regulations as delegated functions rather than independent licensing regimes.
- **Barrier burden analysis has not been run on NYC data** (0 of 8,403 documents analyzed), leaving the NYC licensing burden unquantified.
- The highest-burden licensing-related regulations cluster in **Title 18 (Social Services)** — particularly child welfare, foster care, and child care licensing — where cascading rigidity, one-size-fits-all mandates, and stacked administrative requirements create the most acute compliance challenges.

## 2. NY State Regulatory Licensing Requirements

### 2.1 Home Care Services Licensing (10 NYCRR Parts 760-766)

New York's home care licensing framework operates through two primary tracks, each administered by the Department of Health under Public Health Law Article 36:

#### Certified Home Health Agencies (CHHAs) — Parts 760-763

CHHAs require the most rigorous licensing pathway:

- **Establishment**: Must be approved by the Public Health Council after demonstrating adequate finances, qualified personnel, and public need through a statewide methodology (10 NYCRR Part 760)
- **Certification**: Must obtain a certificate of approval from the commissioner, participate in Medicare/Medicaid, comply with PHL Article 36 (10 NYCRR § 761.2)
- **Operating certificates**: Must be conspicuously posted at principal administrative office; 30 days' advance notice required to patients/families/physicians before surrendering certificate; 90 days' written notice to commissioner (10 NYCRR § 761.2)
- **Service requirements**: Must directly provide at least one service (nursing, HHA, etc.); maintain 24/7 professional telephone consultation; provide part-time nursing/HHA services (10 NYCRR § 763.3)
- **Program changes**: Prior commissioner approval required for any construction/service changes; service additions/deletions regardless of cost; geographic service area changes; method-of-delivery changes exceeding $400K annually; equipment acquisitions exceeding $400K (10 NYCRR § 762.2)

#### Licensed Home Care Services Agencies (LHCSAs) — Part 765

LHCSAs follow a parallel but distinct pathway:

- **Application**: Written application to Public Health and Health Planning Council on department forms, subscribed by CEO/general partner/proprietor (10 NYCRR § 765-1.2)
- **Public need demonstration**: Must satisfy the methodology in § 765-1.16 — the commissioner designates each county as a separate planning area and creates **a rebuttable presumption of no need if 5+ LHCSAs already exist in the county** (10 NYCRR § 765-1.16)
- **Corporate approval**: Separate processes for not-for-profit corporations (§ 765-1.10) and business corporations (§ 765-1.11), each requiring specific documentation under the respective Corporation Law
- **Stock transfers**: Prior Public Health department approval required for transfer of 10%+ of stock or voting rights (§ 765-1.13)
- **Implementation deadline**: Must obtain license and commence operations within 1 year of approval or application is automatically abandoned (§ 765-1.7)
- **Service amendments**: 90 days' advance application required before adding nursing, HHA, or personal care services to an existing license (§ 765-2.2)

#### Personnel Requirements for Both CHHA/LHCSA (10 NYCRR § 766.11)

All home care agencies must:

- Verify qualifications of all health care workers
- Conduct health status assessments for all personnel
- Maintain immunization records: rubella, measles, tuberculosis screening (with annual risk assessments), influenza vaccination (or mask-wearing during flu season)
- Verify employment history and criminal background
- Document professional licenses
- Provide mandatory orientation covering: policies, confidentiality, patient rights, documentation, infection control, emergency procedures, cultural competency
- Deliver annual in-service education: **12 hours for home health aides**, **6 hours for personal care aides**
- Maintain personnel files with all verifications, training records, and health documentation

#### Home Care Worker Registry (10 NYCRR Part 403)

A comprehensive tracking and verification system requiring:

- **Scope**: All agencies certified, licensed, or authorized under PHL Article 36; hospice programs under Article 40; enhanced assisted living residences; education/training programs for advanced HHAs, HHAs, and personal care aides (§ 403.1)
- **Entity responsibilities**: Must designate at least 2 individuals to access/enter registry data; verify registry information for newly trained workers before employment begins; enter employment information within 10 business days (§ 403.3, § 403.5)
- **Training program responsibilities**: Verify trainee identity using government documents; enter trainee information within 10 business days of completion; obtain sworn statements from senior officials certifying identity verification and completion; print department-generated certificates (§ 403.4)
- **Worker responsibilities**: Retain and display training completion certificates; notify programs of registry errors or personal data changes; provide proof of legal name changes (marriage certificate, divorce decree, court order) (§ 403.6)
- **Record retention**: 6 years for all registry-related records

### 2.2 Professional Licensing (8 NYCRR — Education Department)

#### Nursing (8 NYCRR Part 64)

The nursing licensure framework establishes multiple credential tiers:

| Credential | Education | Examination | Additional Requirements |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|------------------------|
| Registered Professional Nurse (RN) | Department-registered program (30-40 semester hours depending on degree); baccalaureate required within 10 years of initial licensure | Single comprehensive exam, all parts in one sitting | Exemption from BSN requirement for nurses licensed before Dec 18, 2017 |
| Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN) | High school graduation + equivalent nursing preparation (min 9 months) | Single approved examination, single score | — |
| Nurse Practitioner | Registered educational program + 3 semester hours pharmacology | Separate certification; specialty area specified on certificate | Prescriptive privileges require additional instruction in prescription/recordkeeping law |
| Clinical Nurse Specialist | Four pathways: registered CNS program; equivalent program + national certification; out-of-state equivalence; pre-September 2015 application with master's | Separate certification application + fee | Unrestricted NY RN license required |
| Advanced Home Health Aide | Approved advanced training program + registry enrollment | — | Registry listing required before providing services |

**Key observation**: An RN who obtains nurse practitioner certification must complete a **separate** certification process from their original RN licensure. A nurse moving into home care must **additionally** comply with the Home Care Worker Registry (Part 403), agency-specific orientation and in-service education (§ 766.11), and potentially a new criminal background check (Part 402) — even though their professional competency was already validated through the Education Department licensing process.

#### Social Work (8 NYCRR Part 74)

| Credential | Education | Examination | Experience | CE |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|------------|-----|
| Licensed Master Social Worker (LMSW) | Master's degree in social work from registered/accredited program | ASWB examination (passing score set by State Board) | — | 36 hours per triennial registration |
| Licensed Clinical Social Worker (LCSW) | Master's degree with 12+ semester hours clinical coursework (diagnosis, assessment, treatment, populations) | Separate ASWB clinical examination | 3 years full-time supervised clinical experience; 2,000+ client contact hours over 36-60 months; 100+ hours face-to-face supervision (individual or group) | 36 hours per triennial registration |

**Supervised experience requirements for LCSW** (8 NYCRR § 74.3, § 74.6):
- Experience in NY must be obtained as an LMSW or limited permit holder
- Supervised experience plan must be submitted to the State Board for Social Work
- Supervision includes individual or group sessions; supervisor must verify experience on department-prescribed forms
- Acceptable practice settings: corporations, partnerships, government agencies, waiver holders, federally authorized entities

**Supervision of BSW holders** (§ 74.7): Individuals with a baccalaureate in social work may perform LMSW services under supervision — meaning "supervisor is available for consultation, assessment, evaluation, has authorized the services, and exercises appropriate oversight."

**Continuing education** (§ 74.10): 36 hours per 3-year registration covering theories, practice, research, ethics, clinical interventions, supervision. Self-study limited to 12 hours per period. Licensees returning from lapsed registration must complete additional hours (1/month of lapse).

#### Other Health Professions

Each profession has its own parallel licensing framework under Title 8:

| Profession | Part | Key Requirements |
|-----------|------|------------------|
| Podiatry | 65 | Two separate exams (basic/clinical sciences + clinical competency); ankle surgery privileges require additional certification with $220 fee |
| Optometry | 66 | Written + practical exams (8 content areas, min 75.0 average); therapeutic pharmaceutical agent certification requires additional clinical training |
| Ophthalmic Dispensing | 67 | 60-semester-hour program or 2-year supervised alternative training + training permit; 18 hours CE per 3-year registration; separate contact lens certification |
| Physical Therapy | 77 | National Physical Therapy Examination; department retains discretion on comparable exams |
| Marriage & Family Therapy | 79-10 | Examination + CE requirements (same structure as social work) |
| Creative Arts Therapy | 79-11 | Examination + endorsement pathway for out-of-state licensees |
| Psychoanalysis | 79-12 | Examination at master's degree level |
| Behavior Analysis | 79-17 | National certification accepted in lieu of separate exam/education/experience; limited permits available |
| Athletic Training | 79-7 | Can take exam before satisfying all education requirements |

### 2.3 Criminal History Record Checks (10 NYCRR Part 402)

Three separate state laws (2005, 2014, 2016) established mandatory criminal history reviews, creating the current framework:

- **Applicability** (§ 402.2): ALL service providers at residential health care facilities, home health agencies, long-term care programs, adult homes, enriched housing programs, residences for adults, and hospice programs licensed under PHL Articles 7, 28, 36, and 40
- **"Employee in direct care and supervision"** defined as: unlicensed personnel with physical access to residents or providing face-to-face care in specified facility types (§ 402.3)
- **Employer obligations** (§ 402.4):
  - Conduct checks in a timely manner
  - Designate authorized personnel to request/receive/review/act on criminal history information
  - Develop written policies defining who is subject to checks
  - Define supervision requirements for temporary employees by facility type
  - Notify department of terminations
  - May NOT require employees to pay for check costs
  - Must protect confidentiality of fingerprinting and criminal history information
- **Pre-check requirements** (§ 402.5): Must obtain written criminal history certifications from applicants, inform applicants of rights to review/correct records, obtain signed informed consent on department-specified form
- **Reimbursement** (§ 402.10): Facilities may claim reimbursement for Division processing fees, FBI check fees, and fingerprinting costs through state funding or Medicaid

**Separate child care criminal history system** (18 NYCRR Part 413): Operates independently with its own 5-year recertification requirement, creating a parallel but non-portable background check regime.

### 2.4 Child Care and Foster Care Licensing (18 NYCRR)

#### Child Day Care Licensing

| Provider Type | Regulation | Application Requirements | Staff Qualifications |
|--------------|-----------|------------------------|---------------------|
| Group Family Day Care | 18 NYCRR Part 416 | Application form, medical statements, training summaries, references, criminal history attestations, fingerprints, child support/workers' comp certifications, child abuse clearances, health/safety verifications, water supply tests, fuel-burning inspections, environmental hazard certifications, residential use proof, health care plans (§ 416.2) | Min age 18; 2 years child care experience or 1 year + 6 hrs training; 2 references; medical statement; criminal history/SCR/SEL clearances (§ 416.13) |
| Family Day Care | 18 NYCRR Part 417 | Similar application package | Same qualification structure as group family (§ 417.13) |
| Child Day Care Centers | 18 NYCRR Part 418 | License application on prescribed forms with required attestations | Training/experience appropriate to function; maturity, good character, suitable qualifications (§§ 418-1.13) |
| School-Age Programs | 18 NYCRR Part 414 | Background reviews, character assessments, criminal history clearances | Directors: associate's degree + 2 yrs experience (1 supervisory); Group teachers: associate's + 1 yr or HS diploma + 2 yrs; Assistant teachers: HS diploma or 1 yr experience (§ 414.13) |
| Small Day Care Centers | 18 NYCRR Part 418-2 | Registration application | Min age 18; 2 yrs experience or 1 yr + 6 hrs training (§§ 418-2.13) |

**Training requirements across all child care types** (§§ 416.14, 417.14, 418-1.14, 418-2.14, 414.14):
- Pre-application orientation required before application issuance
- Federal-compliant health and safety training before licensure or within 3 months
- **30 hours of training every 2 years**, with 15 hours in first 6 months, minimum 5 hours annually
- Required topics: child development, nutrition, child abuse prevention, shaken baby syndrome, adverse childhood experiences/trauma-informed care, SIDS/safe sleep, medication administration, infection control, emergency preparedness
- At least one caregiver must maintain current CPR and first aid certification with in-person competency component

#### Foster Care Certification (18 NYCRR Part 443)

- **Certification standards** (§ 443.3): State/local law compliance, safe/sanitary conditions, sleeping arrangements (max 3/bedroom, separate beds), water/heating/sanitation, fire safety, secure firearm storage
- **Emergency foster homes** (§ 443.7): Expedited approval requires signed agreement, expedited home study, character reference check, agency record review
- **Annual renewal** (§ 443.10): Written evaluations using certification criteria, care quality assessments, physician health statements (if 2+ years since last exam), oral review of training
- **Dual certification** (§ 443.9): Foster parent certification and adoptive parent approval may be pursued simultaneously; adoption study uses same standards plus marital standards from Part 421
- **Emergency foster parent training** (§ 446.5): 15 hours within 4 months of designation, 6 hours yearly covering child development, discipline, communication, family systems, crisis intervention

#### Qualified Residential Treatment Programs (18 NYCRR Part 439)

- Must be operated by authorized agencies
- Implement trauma-informed treatment model aligned with nationally recognized standards
- Maintain **24/7 clinical staffing** (nursing and licensed clinical staff)
- Certification requires written application to OCFS demonstrating compliance with Parts 439, 441, and 442
- OCFS must approve all manuals, policies, and procedures
- **Long stayer provisions** (§ 439.5): 15+ absolute day-specific deadlines across three population tiers with no flexibility or grace period. For children under 13, the entire documentation sequence — independent clinical review, qualified individual/team evaluation, local commissioner certification, system-of-record entry, OCFS submission — must occur within 55 days (day 125 to day 180)

### 2.5 Facility and Institutional Licensing (10 NYCRR Parts 400-405)

#### Operating Certificates (Part 401)

- Initial certificates issued automatically upon final approval of establishment/construction application
- Remain valid until revised, revoked, or surrendered unless expiration date specified
- Each separately incorporated or independently governed facility requires its own certificate
- **Any change** in physical plant, bed capacity, services, or ownership requires prior written DOH approval
- Capital changes exceeding $100,000 require certified cost schedules within 120 days of final construction inspection

#### Integrated Outpatient Services (Part 404)

- Providers must hold operating certificates from **at least two of three agencies**: Department of Health, Office of Mental Health, Office of Alcoholism and Substance Abuse Services
- Three models: Primary Care Host, Mental Health Behavioral Care Host, Comprehensive
- Unannounced inspections using a joint-licensing instrument developed collaboratively by all three agencies
- Staffing requirements include: health status exams prior to employment and annually, immunization records, TB screening with annual risk assessments, influenza vaccination or mask-wearing, identification insignia at all times

#### Nursing Home Staffing (10 NYCRR § 415.13, § 415.26)

- Minimum 3.5 nursing hours per resident per day (2.2 CNA hours, 1.1 RN/LPN hours)
- RN on-site at least 8 consecutive hours daily, 7 days/week
- Full-time director of nursing required
- Administrators must be licensed
- Nurse aide training and certification programs with specific curriculum and competency evaluations
- Non-compliance triggers enforcement actions

## 3. NY State Sub-Regulatory / Guidance Licensing Requirements

Our database contains approximately **685 NY State guidance documents** including GIS (General Information System) letters, DAL (Dear Administrator) letters, ADL letters, and policy guidance documents. These add operational detail and interpretation to the regulatory framework:

- Guidance documents elaborate on licensing procedures, clarify eligibility criteria, specify documentation formats, and interpret regulatory requirements
- They effectively create additional compliance obligations even though they are technically non-binding
- Examples include guidance on home care worker registry procedures, criminal history record check processes, training program approvals, and facility inspection standards

The guidance layer is particularly significant because it often converts regulatory flexibility into rigid operational requirements. Where a regulation might state "adequate training," guidance documents may specify exact hours, approved providers, and documentation formats — creating de facto additional licensing requirements that providers must follow.

## 4. County-Level Licensing Requirements

### What Our Data Shows

Our database does not contain separate county-level regulatory documents. Instead, county requirements appear **embedded within state regulations** as delegated functions. This reflects New York's structure where counties operate under state authority rather than creating independent licensing frameworks.

### County Roles in the Licensing Ecosystem

**Local Health Officers** (10 NYCRR Part 11):
- Must meet state-defined qualifications: licensed physician or non-physician with specified education and supervisory experience in health administration
- County health departments execute state public health mandates rather than creating independent licensing requirements

**Local Health Department Programs** (10 NYCRR Part 40):
- Authorized to run licensing/inspection programs for: radioactive materials (§ 40-2.60), radiation-producing equipment (§ 40-2.61), tanning facilities (§ 40-2.62)
- All programs must be conducted **consistent with state Part 16 standards** — counties cannot create more permissive or more restrictive licensing requirements
- Fee-based permit and inspection services (§ 40-1.51) across environmental health service areas

**LHCSA Public Need Determinations** (10 NYCRR § 765-1.16):
- Each county is designated as a separate planning area
- Rebuttable presumption of no need if 5+ LHCSAs already exist in the county
- This creates county-by-county variation in whether new home care agencies can enter the market, even though the licensing standard itself is uniform

**Local Social Services Districts** (18 NYCRR Parts 300+):
- Administer foster care, child protective services, SNAP, HEAP under state standards
- Local commissioners have authority to review and apply state rules but must file proposed rules with the State Department at least 30 days before effective date (§ 300.6)
- Local departments bear financial responsibility for certain program losses (§ 387.3)

### Key Finding: County Requirements as State-Delegated Functions

Counties in New York do **not** create independent licensing regimes that duplicate state requirements. Instead, they serve as implementation arms of state licensing systems. However, this delegation itself creates burden:

1. **Administrative layers**: Providers must interact with both state and county agencies to complete licensing processes, even though the standards are set at the state level
2. **Variable capacity**: County health departments vary significantly in staffing and resources, creating uneven processing times for the same licensing requirements
3. **Communication gaps**: Changes in state guidance may be implemented at different speeds across counties, creating compliance uncertainty

### 5.2 NYC-Specific Licensing Frameworks

The NYC Health Code creates **distinct licensing layers** that operate alongside — and on top of — state requirements:

#### Child Care Permits (NYC Health Code Article 47)

NYC requires a **separate permit from the NYC Commissioner** before operating any child care program, creating a dual-licensing system where providers must satisfy both state (18 NYCRR Parts 416-418) and city requirements:

- **Pre-permit orientation** required before application (§ 47.09)
- **Application documentation**: Facility blueprints, certificates of occupancy from DOB, fire safety compliance reports from FDNY, written safety plans, staffing plans, health care plans
- **Education directors** must have baccalaureate degree in early childhood education AND State certification, serving no more than 2 groups (§ 47.13)
- **Alternative qualifications** for night programs (§ 47.17): Baccalaureate + 12 credits in ECE + 2 years experience
- **Family shelter programs** (§ 47.18): Designated shelter child supervision liaisons with baccalaureate degree per 30 enrolled children
- **Staff-to-child ratios** range from 1:3-1:4 for infants to 1:15 for ages 5-6 (§ 47.23)
- **Medical requirements**: All children must receive medical examinations including developmental assessment, nutritional evaluation, lead/TB/vision/anemia screening prior to admission (§ 47.25)
- **Health certifications**: All staff must present health care provider certificates **every two years** confirming physical and mental fitness (§ 47.33)
- **Training**: All staff must complete mandatory training within 3 months: child abuse prevention (2 hrs every 24 months), infection control, emergency procedures, hazard identification (§ 47.37)
- **Food service**: Separate food service establishment permit + certified food protection supervisor required during all food operations (§ 47.61)
- **Lead testing**: Mandatory baseline testing within 60 days, then every 5 years; remedial action if levels exceed 15 ppb (§ 47.43)

#### Camps (NYC Health Code Article 48)

- Camp operators must be the permittee
- **Camp directors**: Minimum bachelor's degree (or age 21-25) + 24 weeks administrative/supervisory experience
- **Health directors**: Separate qualification requirements
- **Transportation**: Drivers must be 18+ with valid license and 1 year experience; bus operators must hold current chauffeur's license (§ 48.23)
- Premises must comply with **NYC Building Code AND NYC Zoning Regulations AND obtain certificate of occupancy from DOB AND fire safety statement from FDNY** (§ 48.19)
- Enrollment forms must disclose licensing by NYC DOHMH (§ 48.29)

#### After-School and Youth Centers (NYC Health Code Article 48-A)

- **Directors**: Bachelor's degree + 21 years old + relevant experience
- **Group leaders**: 19+ with 1 year college + 6 months experience
- **Assistants**: 17+ with 3 months experience
- At least one staff member with advanced first aid certification
- Comprehensive facility requirements including compliance with Building Code, zoning, certificates of occupancy, fire safety (§ 48-A.17)

#### Children's Institutions (NYC Health Code Article 51)

- **Separate permit from Commissioner** required unless institution is duly incorporated with Supreme Court approval and State Board of Social Welfare certification (§ 51.03)
- Application requires: certificates of occupancy from Buildings Department, fire safety clearance, maximum capacity by age group, floor plan sketch (§ 51.05)
- Licensed physician on staff with monthly visits minimum (§ 51.19)
- Comprehensive medical examination schedule: monthly under 6 months, every 2 months 6-12 months, every 3 months 1-3 years, semi-annually 3-6 years, annually 6+ years

#### Schools (NYC Health Code Article 49)

- Certificate of occupancy from DOB AND fire safety statement from FDNY required before operating (§ 49.03)
- All children must receive medical examination by licensed physician before/upon admission (§ 49.05)
- Licensed physician must be in charge of health care services (§ 49.15)
- Boarding children under 6 prohibited; ages 6-15 requires written Department approval plus DOB and FDNY approvals (§ 49.11)

## 6. Duplicative and Redundant Licensing Requirements

### 6.1 Criminal Background Checks: Three Non-Portable Systems

The most significant duplication in New York's licensing landscape is the existence of **three separate, non-interoperable criminal history record check systems**:

| System | Authority | Applicability | Recertification |
|--------|-----------|--------------|-----------------|
| Health care facility checks | 10 NYCRR Part 402 (DOH) | Residential health care facilities, home health agencies, long-term care, adult homes, enriched housing, hospice | Per-hire; no portability |
| Child care checks | 18 NYCRR Part 413 (OCFS) | All child day care programs | **5-year recertification** for all personnel including volunteers |
| Justice Center checks | 14 NYCRR (Justice Center) | Programs serving vulnerable populations | Separate process and timeline |

**The Barrier analysis flagged this directly** (18 NYCRR § 449.4, composite burden score 30.11):

> *"Three sequential, mandatory background check workflows (VPCR staff exclusion list, SCR child abuse inquiry, Justice Center criminal history) each with separate forms, prescribed notice requirements, fees, and specific payment instruments create stacked friction that can delay hiring and may functionally preclude rapid staffing for programs serving youth."*

**Reform approach identified**: *"Integrate the three background check workflows into a single unified clearance portal. Allow electronic payment. Create a provisionally supervised employment pathway with documented monitoring for low-risk roles where clearance delay would harm program continuity."*

A worker who passes all three checks at one facility must **repeat the entire process** when moving to another facility, even within the same system. There is no mechanism for a "clearance passport" that travels with the worker.

### 6.2 Nursing Credentials + Home Care Provider Requirements

A registered nurse who completes the full Education Department licensing process (education, examination, clinical competency validation per 8 NYCRR Part 64) must, when working in home care, **additionally** satisfy:

1. **Home Care Worker Registry** enrollment (10 NYCRR Part 403) — identity verification, training documentation, 10-business-day registration timelines
2. **Agency-specific orientation** (10 NYCRR § 766.11) — policies, confidentiality, patient rights, documentation, infection control, emergency procedures, cultural competency
3. **Annual in-service education** (§ 766.11) — 12 hours for home health aides, though professional nurses already maintain CE through their licensing board
4. **Health assessments** — immunization records (rubella, measles, TB, influenza), health status exams — separate from any health documentation maintained for professional licensing
5. **Criminal background check** (10 NYCRR Part 402) — new check at each facility, not portable from previous healthcare employment
6. **Employment history verification** — separate from professional licensing verification

The nursing license **already validates clinical competency** through a rigorous education and examination process supervised by the State Board for Nursing. Yet the home care system treats every nurse as if they need to re-prove their basic qualifications from scratch. The additional layers may serve administrative tracking purposes but do not meaningfully add to patient safety validation.

### 6.3 Social Worker Licensing: Layered Credentialing

The path from social work education to independent clinical practice involves **four distinct credentialing stages**, each with its own administrative overhead:

1. **Master's degree** from registered/accredited program (8 NYCRR § 74.1)
2. **LMSW licensure**: Examination + "good moral character" determination + fee (§ 74.2)
3. **Limited permit or LMSW status**: Required before accumulating supervised clinical hours in NY (§ 74.3, § 74.4)
4. **LCSW licensure**: Second examination + 3 years supervised experience (2,000+ client hours over 36-60 months) + 100+ hours face-to-face supervision + supervisor verification on department forms + "good moral character" determination (§§ 74.2-74.6)

**Then**, to work in specific settings:
- Child welfare (Title 18): Additional training requirements, separate background checks, separate staffing standards
- Mental health facilities (Title 14): Additional facility-specific credentialing
- Schools (Title 8): Additional school service certification

**Supervision documentation burden**: The LCSW supervision requirement creates burden on **both** the candidate and the supervisor. Supervisors must be available for consultation, authorize services, and verify experience on department-prescribed forms. Supervision plans must be submitted to the State Board. This creates a documentation overhead that compounds across every LCSW candidate a supervisor oversees.

### 6.4 "Good Moral Character": A Systemic Undefined Standard

This was the **single most commonly flagged burdensome licensing requirement** in our compliance issue analysis. Our data identified **25+ separate regulations** across 8 NYCRR titles that apply an undefined "good moral character" standard:

| Regulation | Profession/Role | Severity |
|-----------|----------------|----------|

**Common problems across all instances:**

1. **No defined criteria**: None of these regulations define what "good moral character" means or provide evaluation standards
2. **No Article 23-A reference**: New York's Correction Law §§ 750-755 mandates a specific multi-factor balancing test before criminal history can be used to deny licensure. None of these regulations reference it.
3. **No enumerated factors**: No timeframes, no offense relevance requirements, no rehabilitation weighting
4. **Unchecked discretion**: Each regulation grants the licensing authority "sole" or "unchecked" discretion to approve or deny based on character
5. **No appeal pathway**: Most regulations do not define how to challenge a negative character determination
6. **Disproportionate impact**: This vague standard disproportionately affects people with criminal histories, immigrants, people of color, and individuals from marginalized communities

**The particular irony of the "endorsement" cases**: Several of these regulations apply the "good moral character" standard to **licensure-by-endorsement** pathways — meaning applicants who already hold valid, active licenses in other jurisdictions. These applicants have already been vetted by another state's licensing authority, yet NY requires an additional undefined character assessment on top of the existing credential.

### 6.5 CPR Certification: Duplicated Across Professions

CPR certification is required separately by multiple regulations, each specifying its own approved providers and renewal timelines:

- **Dentists** (8 NYCRR § 61.19): In-person CPR from AHA, Red Cross, NSC, or ASHI; specific content requirements
- **Nurses**: Various CPR requirements depending on setting and role
- **Radiologic technologists** (10 NYCRR § 89.40): CPR through "approved organizations"
- **Child care workers** (18 NYCRR §§ 416.14, 417.14): In-person competency component required
- **Camp staff**: First aid certification requirements (NYC Health Code § 48-A)

A single universal CPR certification — recognized across all provider types — would eliminate the need for profession-specific CPR requirements and reduce both cost and administrative overhead.

### 6.6 Training Recertification Misalignment

Different regulatory bodies impose training requirements on the **same topics** at **different intervals**:

| Topic | Child Care (18 NYCRR) | NYC Health Code | Professional Licensing (8 NYCRR) |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| Child abuse prevention | 30 hrs/2 yrs (among other topics) | 2 hrs every 24 months | Part of CE requirements |
| Infection control | Included in 30 hrs/2 yrs | Within 3 months of hire + ongoing | Facility-specific |
| CPR/First aid | Current certification required | Current certification required | Profession-specific |
| Medication administration | 3-year recertification | Within 3 months | N/A |
| Shaken baby syndrome | Included in 30 hrs/2 yrs | N/A | N/A |

A provider operating under both state and city authority must track multiple overlapping training schedules, potentially completing substantially similar training under different regulatory frameworks.

### 7.2 Highest-Burden Licensing-Related Regulations

The following regulations scored highest on the composite burden index for licensing-related issues:

#### 18 NYCRR § 430.12 — Diligence of Effort (Foster Care) — Burden: 59.45

> *"The section stacks numerous procedural friction points: 2-week written notices, mandatory face-to-face contacts within 30 days of missed reviews, 90-day written discharge notices, biweekly visitation planning requirements, mandatory case conferences with 11 enumerated potential participants, and 60-day petition deadlines."*

**One-size-fits-all problem**: Service plan review requirements apply identically to all foster care cases regardless of stability or proximity to permanency. A child in a stable long-term placement weeks from adoption faces the same full panel review as a crisis placement.

**Reform approach**: Introduce tiered review requirements based on case stability. Cases in active crisis get full panel reviews; pre-finalization cases use streamlined documentation and smaller panels.

#### 18 NYCRR § 423.5 — Fiscal Requirements (Preventive Services) — Burden: 55.05

> *"Districts must simultaneously maximize preventive services, avoid 'over-utilization,' meet performance standards, explore alternative funding, and maintain baseline spending levels without guidance on prioritization."*

**Cascade of rigidity**: Non-supplantation certification, multiple baseline comparisons to frozen fiscal years, and complex fund tracking create significant documentation and accounting burden.

#### 18 NYCRR § 439.5 — Long Stayers (QRTPs) — Burden: 54.11

> *"15+ absolute day-specific deadlines across three population tiers with no flexibility, force majeure provision, or prioritization guidance. The compressed 10-day windows between submission (day 310/320) and OCFS determination (day 365) leave no margin for real-world delays."*

**Reform approach**: Replace hard day-specific deadlines with deadline windows and add grace period provisions for documented emergencies.

#### 18 NYCRR § 417.11 — Health and Infection Control (Family Day Care) — Burden: 53.75

> *"Applies identical medication administration training, health care consultant, and documentation requirements to all family day care homes regardless of whether they care for 2 children or 12, imposing disproportionate compliance costs on small home-based providers."*

**Unfunded mandate**: Requires expensive medication administration training programs and health care consultant services without providing funding or reimbursement mechanisms.

**Reform approach**: Create tiered requirements based on program size. Allow group/shared health care consultants for small providers; offer state-funded training modules and virtual consultant services.

#### 18 NYCRR § 416.11 — Health and Infection Control (Group Family Day Care) — Burden: 52.94

> *"Multiple medical statements required within rigid 12-month windows, no grace periods, automatic exclusion if documentation lapses."*

**Reform approach**: Allow reasonable grace periods (30-60 days) for documentation renewal; permit provisional enrollment pending documentation for low-risk situations.

#### 18 NYCRR § 430.11 — Appropriateness of Placement — Burden: 39.24

> *"The section triggers a distinct documentation obligation for nearly every subsection — continuity (7 separate items), group homes (multiple), institutions, supervised independent living — creating a stacked, repetitive paperwork regime at case opening."*

**Reform approach**: Consolidate all placement-entry documentation into a single structured form capturing all required elements in one submission rather than triggering separate forms per subsection.

#### Individual Practitioners

**Cost burden:**
- Professional licensing examination fees: Vary by profession ($115-$220+ per credential)
- Triennial registration renewals
- Continuing education costs (36 hours per 3-year period for social workers; varies by profession)
- Multiple CPR certifications if working across settings

**Complexity burden:**
- Multiple credentialing bodies (Education Department for professional license, DOH for facility-specific requirements, OCFS for child care roles)
- Separate application processes for each credential tier
- "Good moral character" evaluations with no defined standards or predictable outcomes
- Multiple overlapping supervision documentation requirements

- 90-day advance application for any service amendment

- Criminal background checks for every hire (no portability)

- Minimum 2 designated registry access individuals
- 10-business-day reporting windows for multiple events
- Written policies for registry implementation

## 8. Reform Recommendations

### 8.1 Portable Background Check System

**Problem**: Three separate criminal history check systems (DOH, OCFS, Justice Center) with no interoperability. Workers must repeat checks when changing employers.

**Recommendation**: Create a unified "Health and Human Services Background Clearance" that:
- Integrates VPCR, SCR, and Justice Center checks into a single portal
- Issues a portable digital clearance credential valid across all licensed facility types
- Allows electronic payment and submission
- Establishes a "provisionally supervised employment" pathway for low-risk roles during clearance processing
- Sets a defined validity period (e.g., 2 years) with continuous monitoring for new offenses rather than periodic recertification

### 8.2 Standardize "Good Moral Character"

**Problem**: 25+ regulations apply an undefined "good moral character" standard with no criteria, no Article 23-A reference, and unchecked discretion.

**Recommendation**:
- Replace all instances of "good moral character as determined by the department" with a defined evaluation framework that:
  - References Article 23-A (Correction Law §§ 750-755) mandatory balancing factors
  - Specifies relevant offense categories and timeframes
  - Requires documented rehabilitation consideration
  - Provides a defined appeal pathway
  - Establishes consistent standards across all professions
- This could be accomplished through a single cross-cutting regulation rather than amending 25+ separate provisions

### 8.3 Credential Reciprocity and Subsumption

**Problem**: Licensed professionals must re-prove basic qualifications when moving between care settings.

**Recommendation**:
- Establish that certain credentials logically subsume lower-level requirements:
  - RN licensure should satisfy home care worker registry requirements (or dramatically streamline them)
  - A valid LCSW should not require additional "good moral character" review for facility-specific roles
  - National board certification should be accepted in lieu of state-specific examinations (as already done for behavior analysts under 8 NYCRR §§ 79-17.6)
- Create a "healthcare professional credential passport" that eliminates redundant verification of credentials already validated by the Education Department

### 8.4 Tiered Requirements by Provider Size

**Problem**: Identical requirements regardless of whether a provider serves 2 people or 200.

**Recommendation** (from Barrier analysis):
- Create tiered compliance frameworks based on program size and complexity
- Small home-based providers (fewer than 6 children) should have:
  - Simplified training requirements
  - Shared/virtual health care consultant access
  - Streamlined documentation
  - Reduced consultant visit frequency
- Maintain full requirements for larger institutional providers
- Scale documentation requirements to number of people served

### 8.5 Align Training and Recertification Cycles

**Problem**: Multiple overlapping training mandates from different regulatory bodies on the same topics at different intervals.

**Recommendation**:
- Create a single "provider training registry" that tracks all completed training
- Allow training completed for one regulatory purpose to satisfy requirements of another when topics overlap
- Harmonize recertification cycles across state and city requirements
- Establish universal CPR certification accepted across all provider types and settings

### 8.6 Modernize Submission and Payment Methods

**Problem**: Licensing processes still require certified mail, wet signatures, physical copies, check-only payments, and in-person appearances.

**Recommendation** (supported by compliance issue analysis):
- Accept electronic signatures per ESRA (Electronic Signatures and Records Act) across all licensing applications
- Allow electronic payment for all licensing fees
- Enable online submission of licensing applications and renewals
- Replace mandatory in-person hearings with video conference options (as California already does for gaming license hearings)
- Eliminate certified mail requirements for routine notices

### 8.7 Run Barrier Analysis on NYC Data

**Problem**: No burden analysis exists for 8,403 NYC regulatory documents, leaving the NYC licensing overlay unquantified.

**Recommendation**: Run the Barrier pipeline on NYC jurisdiction data to:
- Quantify the burden of NYC-specific licensing requirements
- Enable direct comparison of NYC vs. state burden scores
- Identify NYC-specific reform opportunities
- Document the cumulative burden on providers subject to both NYC and state licensing

### 8.8 Consolidate Placement and Licensing Documentation

**Problem**: Multiple separate documentation forms and processes triggered at each stage of licensing and placement decisions.

**Recommendation** (from Barrier analysis):
- Consolidate placement-entry documentation into single structured forms
- Replace open-ended narrative requirements with structured electronic forms with checkboxes and brief narrative fields
- Accept existing medical/court records to satisfy documentation requirements without requiring separate narrative restatement
- Create standardized templates for common documentation requirements

### 8.9 Outcome-Based Licensing Standards

**Problem**: Licensing systems focus on process compliance rather than provider quality outcomes.

**Recommendation**:
- Supplement procedural licensing requirements with outcome-based accountability measures
- Define what a certified/licensed program must substantively achieve (e.g., placement stability, health outcomes, educational outcomes)
- Replace prescriptive step-by-step mandates with clear outcome goals and allow providers flexibility in how they achieve them
- Tie licensing renewal to demonstrated outcomes rather than solely procedural compliance

### 8.10 Address Certificate of Need Barriers

**Problem**: LHCSA public need determination creates barriers to entry that entrench existing providers and limit competition.

**Recommendation**:
- Evaluate whether the rebuttable presumption of no need (5+ agencies per county) still serves its intended purpose
- Consider transitioning from certificate-of-need to a registration-based system with quality standards
- If CON is retained, modernize the public need methodology to account for unmet demand, quality gaps, and geographic access challenges within counties
